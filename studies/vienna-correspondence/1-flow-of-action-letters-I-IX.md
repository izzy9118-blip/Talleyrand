# PART ONE — THE FLOW OF ACTION, LETTERS I–IX

The primary object. Actions in sequence, with dates, and what each produced.

26 SEPT (I). Arrives. Reports the field. Proposes, as procedural
housekeeping, a first commission of SIX great powers including France and
Spain — France's readmission to the directorate of Europe advanced as an
administrative suggestion. [DF]
  - Registers Saxony's exclusion from the German commission as arithmetic
    ("I should have wished the number to be seven"). [DF][V]
  - Announces intent to contest Russian ministerial policy in Alexander's
    presence, framed as a hope, so that royal silence licenses it. [DF]

29 SEPT (II). Diagnostic letter. No operation. Assessments taken that later
become levers: Castlereagh lacks decision, is made timid by Parliament ("I
will do all that in me lies to inspire him with firmness"); Metternich
frivolous to the point of calamity; Stadion displaced and therefore
communicative. Requests an audience with Alexander through Nesselrode; no
answer. Reads the non-answer as evidence his principles bite. [DF/SI]
  - Declared strategy: total publicity of principle ("of which we make no
    secret"). [DF]
  - Floats a unanimous declaration by the great powers as a sufficient
    instrument, wrapped in incredulity so it is not formally proposed. [DF]

30 SEPT (III, reporting). THE PRELIMINARY CONFERENCE. The pivotal action of
the opening phase.
  - Invitation reads "assist" at a conference where the four are "met." He
    rewrites the grammar in his reply and inserts SPAIN into the list of
    ministers he will attend with. [DF][V]
  - Coordinates with Labrador in advance: combine what the others want
    divided, separate what they want linked. [DF]
  - Takes the vacant seat between Castlereagh and Metternich. [DF]
  - Destroys the "Secretaries of State only" pretext by counterexample
    (Labrador; Humboldt; the infirmities line). [DF]
  - Challenges "allies" in every paragraph of their protocol: where are we,
    Chaumont or Laon, is there a quarrel and with whom. They plead brevity;
    he answers that brevity must not be purchased at the expense of
    accuracy. [DF][V]
  - Annihilates the interval between the treaty's stipulated date and the
    day the Congress should have met: "All that has been done in the
    interval is foreign to me, and does not exist for me." [DF][V]
  - On Naples: "What King of Naples? We do not know the man in question."
    On the guarantee: those who guaranteed "ought not to have done so, and
    consequently could not." Measures the effect visibly produced and
    deliberately does not prolong it. [DF][V]
  - Feigns incomprehension, reads the paragraphs twice "with the air of"
    striving to understand — and reports the performance as performance to
    the King. [DF]
  RESULT: the protocol is withdrawn. Corroborated by Gentz's diary: the
  intervention hopelessly upset the four's plans; roughly two hours of
  vehement protest; a scene he would never forget. Labrador's "then why did
  you sign it" appears in both accounts and is credited to Labrador in the
  dispatch. [DF, externally corroborated]
  REGISTER DIVERGENCE: dispatch renders cool surgery; the diary records heat
  and duration. Facts check; presentation composed. [SI]

1 OCT (IV, reporting). Converts the victory into a written instrument:
refuses to leave the exchange as untraced conversation and sends a signed
official NOTE, on the ground that the four hold signed conferences among
themselves. Choice of battlefield: paper, where signatures bind. [DF]
  - The four take the line of being offended, having no argument. Their
    protest ("the object is to disunite us") confesses the league. [DF]
  - Battle over inserting PUBLIC LAW into the declaration. Metternich
    proposes settling it by vote — betraying what use they would have made
    of the power they sought. They yield; the phrase lands one sentence
    earlier than he asked. [DF]
  - Grades the victory soberly to the department: "we are HELD to have
    achieved a victory... this will make you understand the spirit that
    animates the Congress." [ED-PRIMARY][DF]

EARLY OCT (audience with Alexander, reported in IV).
  - Answers the Emperor's audit of France's condition by arming France
    verbally: 130,000 ready, 300,000 on summons. [HT]
  - Smuggles counsel to his own King into reported dialogue: Ney frets about
    his endowments; "Your Majesty might diminish his regrets." [DF]
  - Alexander probes for a seam between minister and King; finds it closed.
    [HT]
  - Doctrinal exchange: right first, convenances after. Theatrical
    performance (head against panelling, "Europe, unhappy Europe!", dropped
    arms, silence) extracts the naked declaration: "Rather war than that I
    should renounce what I hold." [HT]
  - Stage directions disclosed to the King in full. [DF]

8 OCT. Last meeting of the eight for some time.

13 OCT (VI). Reports that the four have stopped convening; business now
proceeds by phrase-changes passed through Gentz — the machinery wrecked and
the contest moved onto documents. [DF]
  - JACOBI-KLOEST: asked to concede Warsaw to Alexander, demands a formal
    deed of surrender FROM THE KING OF SAXONY, on the ground of cessions and
    treaties Prussia herself signed. Jacobi discovers the title aloud: "It
    is true, the duchy does belong to him!" One conversation mines both
    Russia's Polish claim and Prussia's Saxon claim with Prussia's own seal.
    [HT — high value, corroboration wanted]
  - THE ELBA FILE. (a) Reports the Treaty of 11 April unexecuted by France;
    Castlereagh has raised it; Alexander has noticed the budget's silence;
    presses the King to act. (b) Reports a forming intention to move
    Bonaparte, with no destination chosen, and proposes the AZORES, five
    hundred leagues from any coast. [DF]
  - Catalogues Alexander's performances (Wagram excursion, Pesth ball in
    Hungarian costume, the sister's tomb with Greeks assembled) as policy,
    not gossip. [DF]
  - STRATEGIC DIAGNOSIS: the union of the four "is more apparent than real,
    and depends solely on the fact that some of them do not choose to
    believe that we have the means to act, while the others do not believe
    that we have the will." [DF] — the organizing sentence of the campaign.

17 OCT (VIII). THE WAR-POWERS REQUEST. The decisive forward action of the
period.
  - Reads Germany's condition: mediatized princes reduced to subjects,
    universities and their young men, unity as a fanatical religion
    infecting even reigning princes, occupations and fresh sacrifices;
    concludes that delay favors subversion and that an adjourned or
    indecisive Congress risks explosion. Refuses to calculate its progress
    on the scale of the French revolution, the carrier class being
    different. Derives a TEMPO requirement: accelerate and end. [DF]
  - Deterrence doctrine: war unwanted, "but in my opinion it would suffice
    to HINT at it, and we should not require to make it"; the fear of war
    ought not to prevail over the fear of a greater evil preventable only by
    war. Coalition arithmetic: Russia and Prussia will not face Austria,
    France, Sardinia, Bavaria and much of Germany. [DF][V]
  - Diagnoses Austria: immense forces, the desire, no firmness of will,
    afraid of Italy — "she would like to be able to count upon our
    co-operation, and does not believe that she can." [DF]
  - SCRIPTS THE FUTURE ASK: predicts Metternich will ask whether and to what
    extent Austria may count on France; states that general assurances are
    already covered but a precise promise requires authorization and special
    instructions; entreats them now; promises restraint; predicts he will
    not need them. [DF] — the 3 January treaty requested eleven weeks early.
  - CASTLEREAGH'S MEMORANDUM SHOWN TO HIM: France inside England's paper.
    Reads Metternich's involuntary surprise as documentary proof that the
    four had agreed not to communicate with outsiders. Types his own
    inference: "I cannot doubt, although neither one nor the other has told
    me so." [DF]
  - The Memorandum quotes the 1813 treaty barring French interference in
    Poland; Castlereagh hastens to gloss it as meaning the France of 1813,
    not of today. Talleyrand records clause and gloss together. [DF]
  - Assessment: Castlereagh sound on Poland, unsound on Saxony; "principles
    do not appear to be his strong point." [DF][V]
  - [ED-PRIMARY] Department register, same period: England fright-driven,
    wants northern powers strong enough to fence French trade, "they lend
    themselves to all that Prussia demands." The dogs taunt appears polished
    for the King ("you talk cleverly but do not want to act") and raw for the
    department ("you appear to us to be dogs, who bark very loud, but you do
    not bite").
  - [ED-PRIMARY] NOAILLES, 9 NOV, reporting the King of Bavaria: the French
    envoys have been closely watched, and it was discovered WITH MUCH
    SURPRISE that they avoid all secret manoeuvres, have spent no money, and
    that their conduct is stainless and free from intrigue — "I have made a
    protest respecting the affairs of Saxony. I am with you." External
    verification, from hostile surveillance, that the candor strategy
    produced alignment.

19 OCT (IX). THE ISOLATION CAMPAIGN AND THE COUNTER-CONSTRUCTION.
  - Labrador reproached by the four as a turncoat for agreeing with him and
    frequenting his house; Labrador holds his opinion but visits less.
    Talleyrand reads the pressure on the periphery as a gauge of the
    campaign's force on less independent ministers. [DF]
  - The federal-constitution commissioners are sworn to secrecy, "especially
    directed against the French Legation. The plan is now to isolate it, as
    it has been found impossible to make it accept the role proposed to it."
    He calls the precaution useless — then demonstrates it by producing a
    full account of the four's design. [DF]
  - COUNTER-CONSTRUCTION (from "a man whose position affords him an
    opportunity of acquiring accurate information"): the four's wartime
    alliance survived as spirit; the Congress was convened only to lend
    their arrangements an apparent sanction; France could not be excluded
    after the Restoration, so she is to be set aside practically; the design
    is at bottom England's — "her principles are her interests" — aimed at
    keeping the French navy weak, isolating France from naval powers (the 5
    July treaty with Spain barring the family compact), and pinning her to
    the continent by an Austria-Prussia bloc, which requires a strong
    Prussia, which requires Saxony. Stewart's appointment read as evidence.
    Castlereagh's Poland Memorandum re-read by function: an alibi for
    Parliament to excuse not saving Saxony. [HT — analysis, explicitly typed
    by him as probable, not affirmed]
  - Models opposition as possibly manufactured: the phantom Poland may have
    been suggested to Alexander by those now opposing it; his promises to the
    Poles may be ones he would regret keeping; the resistance he meets may be
    what he most desires. [HT]
  - Metternich "is himself set in motion without knowing it... the mere tool
    of the intrigues of which he believes himself the leader." [HT][V]
  - EXPLICIT TYPING: "Without affirming that all this information is
    perfectly exact, I may say that it appears to me extremely probable."
    [DF] — native proposition-typing at full scale.
  - Two fêtes: the Legation cannot attend the Leipzig commemoration (a
    celebration of France's defeat) and does attend Metternich's peace ball,
    where the work is done. Lays reflections before the Emperor of Austria at
    a preceding ball; times Castlereagh's conversation with the Emperor at
    twenty minutes and ascertains its subject. [DF]
