# PART FOUR — OPEN QUESTIONS

  Q-1. THE ROYAL BACK-CHANNEL. Tertiary sources state Louis XVIII distrusted
  Talleyrand and negotiated with Metternich by mail. Unverified. If true, it
  re-reads the entire correspondence. High consequence. [WH]

  Q-2. PATERNITY OF DOCTRINE. The King is no instrument: he states method
  ("I put things at the worst, because I think that is the true way in which
  to reason"), produces his own strategic survey, and states the
  non-sanction doctrine in dynastic key with a dare attached. Who taught
  whom cannot be assumed. Must remain open in any deed. [UU]

  Q-3. AUDIENCE LAYERING. The dispatches have several readers (the King,
  Blacas, the department, couriers, the archive). Whether they carry a
  surface for the court and a teaching for the King alone is unsettled.
  Note the two-register evidence: the dogs taunt polished for the King and
  raw for the department; the Metternich condemnation delivered unnamed
  while the praise is named. [UU]

  Q-4. THE INFORMANT IN LETTER IX. Whether the source is wholly real, or
  partly a device for distancing the analysis's cynicism from himself,
  cannot be settled from the text. [UU]

  Q-5. HIS MODEL OF THE FRENCH REVOLUTION. Never stated; one fragment leaks
  under argumentative load (Jacobinism located in the middle and lower
  classes; the whole compressed under one name; dated to 1789). Collect
  fragments as they surface; never presume the content. Weight: low for
  deeds; it is a trace about him, not a load-bearing member of his argument.
  [UU]
