# Memoirs Unit 07 — printed pp. 25–28
## Library, circle, and disciplined ignorance

**Primary witness:** *Memoirs of the Prince de Talleyrand*, Vol. I, English translation by Raphael Ledos de Beaufort, edited by the Duc de Broglie (G. P. Putnam’s Sons, 1891).

## 1. Textual ground

> “My first care was to form a library, which became valuable eventually by the care with which the works composing it were selected…”
>
> — printed p. 25 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand makes formation of a deliberately selected library his first act after gaining freedom to order his own life.

### Abstraction

**MAKE A CURATED LIBRARY AN EARLY ACT OF PREPARATION.**

## 2. Textual ground

> “I sought to make the acquaintance of the men most renowned for their past life, their works, their ambition, or the prospects held out to them by their birth, their friends or their talents.”
>
> — printed p. 25 `[V]`

### Local reading

His human field is prospective as well as retrospective: he studies established figures and people likely to matter because of position, networks, ambition, or talent.

### Abstraction

**BUILD A HUMAN LIBRARY AS WELL AS A BOOK LIBRARY.**

**STUDY TOMORROW’S ACTORS BEFORE THEY BECOME TODAY’S OFFICEHOLDERS.**


## 3. Textual ground

> “We touched upon every subject and with the greatest liberty… These conversations were both pleasant and instructive…”
>
> — printed p. 28 `[V]`

### Local reading

He describes unrestricted conversation across many subjects as both pleasant and instructive. The sentence does not say that a verdict had to follow each conversation.

### Abstraction

**FREE CONVERSATION ACROSS MANY SUBJECTS CAN ITSELF BE POLITICAL INSTRUCTION.**

## 4. Textual ground

> “The news of the day, political, commercial, administrative, and financial questions all in succession furnished topics of conversation.”
>
> — printed p. 28 `[V]`

### Local reading

The circle’s knowledge field is plural and practical; political judgment is fed by several domains that touch the same board.

### Abstraction

**CONSULT ACROSS DOMAINS WHEN THE POLITICAL QUESTION CROSSES THEM.**


## 5. Textual ground

> “As for us ignorant ones, Lauzun, Barthès, Choiseul and myself, though anxious to learn, we confined ourselves to generalities.”
>
> — printed pp. 28–29 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand distinguishes the men with special knowledge from “us ignorant ones,” while stressing that the latter remained anxious to learn and restricted themselves to generalities.

### Abstraction

**KNOW WHO KNOWS WHAT.**

**ACKNOWLEDGE WHEN YOU LACK SPECIAL KNOWLEDGE; REMAIN ANXIOUS TO LEARN.**

### Limit / alternative

The passage does not say the non-expert may ignore specialists; it shows the need to keep jurisdiction of knowledge clear.
