# Memoirs Unit 22 — printed pp. 98–102
## Principle, claim, and constitutional consequence

**Primary witness:** *Memoirs of the Prince de Talleyrand*, Vol. I, English translation by Raphael Ledos de Beaufort, edited by the Duc de Broglie (G. P. Putnam’s Sons, 1891).

**Witness condition:** English translation, not quotation-grade French. All quoted wording is `[V]`: exact wording of the current English project witness, usable to ground sequence, distinctions, and abstraction, but not to license exact French voice imitation.

**Quotation status:** `QUOTATION_GROUNDED`

**Evidence status:** Independent Memoirs reconstruction. No preexisting deed was consulted or cross-referenced.

## 1. Textual ground

> “The incompatibility of an hereditary monarchy with such a theory was flagrant. However, the Assembly wished, in good faith, to preserve the monarchy…”
>
> — printed p. 98 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand explains a structural contradiction without needing to invent bad faith in the actors who held both sides of it.

### Abstraction

**DO NOT INVENT BAD FAITH TO EXPLAIN A STRUCTURAL CONTRADICTION.**

## 2. Textual ground

> “You hold as a principle that the sovereignty belongs to the people. You hold in fact that it has delegated to you the exercise of it in its plenitude. I have my doubts upon that…”
>
> — printed p. 99 `[V]`

### Local reading

He accepts the opponent’s governing premise for the sake of argument, then isolates the separate factual claim of delegation that still requires proof.

### Abstraction

**DISTINGUISH THE SOURCE OF AUTHORITY FROM PROOF THAT A PARTICULAR AGENT RECEIVED THAT AUTHORITY.**

**USE THE OTHER SIDE’S PREMISE ONLY WHERE IT ACTUALLY GOVERNS THE CLAIM.**

## 3. Textual ground

> “I do not pretend to make myself the judge; neither can you be; but this people is a judge that you cannot challenge.”
>
> — printed p. 99 `[V]`

### Local reading

Neither claimant should judge its own authority claim; he seeks a forum competent under the premise both sides invoke.

### Abstraction

**SEEK A COMPETENT TEST WHOSE LEGITIMACY FOLLOWS FROM THE CLAIMED SOURCE OF AUTHORITY.**

## 4. Textual ground

> “rendering it, from being present and absolute as it was, contingent and simply possible.”
>
> — printed pp. 99–100 `[V]`

### Local reading

The proposed appeal does not create the danger; it attempts to change an already operative claim into a contingent one by forcing a test.

### Abstraction

**WHEN A DANGEROUS PREMISE IS ALREADY OPERATIVE, DISTINGUISH RECOGNIZING THE RISK FROM CREATING IT.**

## 5. Textual ground

> “simply reveal the magnitude of the evil”
>
> — printed p. 100 `[V]`

### Local reading

An unfavorable result can improve strategy by destroying false beliefs about the option set.

### Abstraction

**BAD NEWS CAN BE VALUABLE WHEN IT DESTROYS FALSE OPTIONS.**

## 6. Textual ground

> “make it no longer possible to entertain any delusion as to the real nature of this evil.”
>
> — printed p. 100 `[V]`

### Local reading

The test is valuable under more than one outcome because even defeat yields decision-relevant information.

### Abstraction

**PREFER TESTS THAT IMPROVE THE DECISION UNDER MULTIPLE PLAUSIBLE RESULTS.**

## 7. Textual ground

> “trusted entirely to intrigue to destroy a power that they had allowed to become too strong…”
>
> — printed p. 100 `[V]`

### Local reading

The instrument is no longer proportionate to the target. A tactic that might have worked earlier has lost relevance after the target’s power changed.

### Abstraction

**REPRICE THE INSTRUMENT AFTER THE TARGET HAS CHANGED.**

## 8. Textual ground

> “Those who foresaw its ravages most clearly, were compelled to confine themselves as much as prudence allowed, to playing a passive part. This I resolved to do on most questions. However, I felt that I ought to speak on several matters…”
>
> — printed p. 102 `[V]`

### Local reading

Passive posture is selective rather than universal; he still intervenes where bounded leverage remains.

### Abstraction

**WHEN CONTROL OF THE WHOLE IS LOST, DO NOT CONFUSE SELECTIVE NONINTERVENTION WITH UNIVERSAL INACTION.**

## Reflexive obligation for reconstructing Talleyrand

Under Keel Principle 11, this unit also becomes a research instruction for Talleyrand himself. The repository must test whether Talleyrand himself consistently separated principles from claims of delegated authority and whether his own institutional arguments used opponents’ premises fairly rather than sophistically.

## Build harvest

- **DO NOT INVENT BAD FAITH FOR STRUCTURAL CONTRADICTION.**
- **DISTINGUISH SOURCE OF AUTHORITY FROM PROOF OF DELEGATION.**
- **SEEK A COMPETENT TEST.**
- **DISTINGUISH ACKNOWLEDGING AN EXISTING RISK FROM CREATING IT.**
- **PREFER TESTS THAT IMPROVE DECISION UNDER MULTIPLE OUTCOMES.**
- **BAD NEWS CAN DESTROY FALSE OPTIONS.**
- **REPRICE THE INSTRUMENT AFTER THE TARGET CHANGES.**
- **SELECTIVE NONINTERVENTION IS NOT UNIVERSAL INACTION.**
