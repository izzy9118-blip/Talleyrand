# Memoirs Unit 28 — printed pp. 125–129a
## Activity, purpose, reputation, and public duty

**Primary witness:** *Memoirs of the Prince de Talleyrand*, Vol. I, English translation by Raphael Ledos de Beaufort, edited by the Duc de Broglie (G. P. Putnam’s Sons, 1891).

**Witness condition:** English translation, not quotation-grade French. All quoted wording is `[V]`: exact wording of the current English project witness, usable to ground sequence, distinctions, and abstraction, but not to license exact French voice imitation.

**Quotation status:** `AUDITED_QUOTATION_GROUNDED`

**Evidence status:** Independent Memoirs reconstruction. No preexisting deed was consulted or cross-referenced.

## 1. Textual ground

> “All the youth of the Duc d’Orléans was spent without plans, without projects, without result, without discretion.”
>
> — printed p. 125 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand’s criticism is not inactivity but the absence of plan, project, result, and discretion across an active youth.

### Abstraction

**DO NOT CONFUSE A BUSY LIFE WITH A DIRECTED LIFE.**

## 2. Textual ground

> “To instruct himself he went to see the experiments of Préval; he ascended in a balloon; he assisted Cagliostro and the Chevalier de Luxembourg in their fantasmagoria; he went to the Newmarket races and to other fashionable places.”
>
> — printed p. 125 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand says these exposures were undertaken “to instruct himself,” while his surrounding judgment still describes the youth as without plan, project, result, or discretion. The text therefore distinguishes an intention to be instructed from demonstrated formation.

### Abstraction

**DISTINGUISH EXPOSURE UNDERTAKEN FOR INSTRUCTION FROM DEMONSTRATED LEARNING.**

## 3. Textual ground

> “To increase his fortune, which was already immense, he made speculations…”
>
> — printed p. 125 `[V]`

### Local reading

Material need does not explain the continued acquisition; the biographer must ask what other end the acquisition serves.

### Abstraction

**WHEN ACQUISITION CONTINUES AFTER MATERIAL NEED, ASK WHAT END THE ADDITIONAL ACQUISITION SERVES.**


## 4. Textual ground

> “To prove his courage, he had himself applauded at some of the theatres and crowned under the windows of Mademoiselle Arnould.”
>
> — printed p. 126 `[V]`

### Local reading

The Duc attempts to answer doubt about courage with arranged applause and ceremonial acclaim, neither of which is evidence of courage itself.

### Abstraction

**DO NOT TREAT STAGED ACCLAMATION AS PROOF OF THE QUALITY BEING DISPUTED.**

## 5. Textual ground

> “The French people had been called, by the government itself, to consider the situation of the finances, and to hear the statement of the fortune of the State. A light so new to their eyes had caused a lively sensation and made a profound impression. An entirely new power was created in France, that of public opinion.”
>
> — printed p. 127 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand directly links governmental disclosure of the State’s financial condition to the appearance of public opinion as a new power.

### Abstraction

**ASK WHETHER NEW PUBLIC ACCESS TO STATE INFORMATION HAS CREATED A NEW POLITICAL POWER.**


## 6. Textual ground

> “He only made himself noticed by his indifference and lack of application. Diligence at the sessions would have required the sacrifice of his pleasures or of his habits for a considerable time, and this he was not capable of making.”
>
> — printed p. 127 `[V]`

### Local reading

Office tests whether capacity survives sustained application when duty competes with habit and pleasure.

### Abstraction

**TEST CAPACITY BY SUSTAINED APPLICATION WHEN DUTY COMPETES WITH HABIT, PLEASURE, OR CONVENIENCE.**


## 7. Textual ground

> “He pushed his frivolity to the extent of going, during one of the sessions, to a hunt in the woods of Raincy.”
>
> — printed p. 128 `[V]`

### Local reading

The hunt matters in this sequence because it occurs during a session at which the Duc is supposed to be exercising a public responsibility.

### Abstraction

**JUDGE A PRIVATE HABIT POLITICALLY WHEN IT DISPLACES THE PUBLIC DUTY THE ACTOR IS SUPPOSED TO PERFORM.**


## 8. Textual ground

> “This negative praise was not flattering; for was there no other part for the Duc d’Orléans to play, on this memorable occasion, but that of an intriguer?”
>
> — printed p. 128 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand rejects a defense that consists only in saying the Duc did not intrigue; he asks what positive role was available instead.

### Abstraction

**DO NOT CONFUSE NOT DOING THE BAD THING WITH DOING THE REQUIRED THING.**

## 9. Textual ground

> “that a prince of the blood, so foreign to interests of this nature, should not have experienced the noble temptation to crush all these petty intrigues under the weight of his independence… I cannot conceive…”
>
> — printed p. 128 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand judges the Duc partly by practically available independence left unused.

### Abstraction

**COUNT PRACTICALLY AVAILABLE BUT UNUSED CAPACITY IN THE CONDUCT FILE.**

### Limit / alternative

The inference is only fair after establishing that the independence and alternative really existed; Talleyrand’s normative expectation is not self-proving.


## Reflexive obligation for reconstructing Talleyrand

Under Keel Principle 11, this unit also becomes a research instruction for Talleyrand himself. The repository must not call Talleyrand learned because he saw much, active because he moved much, or capable because he was near power. It must trace what he learned, what he sustained, what he used, and what real capacity he left unused.
