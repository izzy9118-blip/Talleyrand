# Memoirs Unit 15 — printed pp. 61–66
## Repairing categories, doubt, and analysis

**Primary witness:** *Memoirs of the Prince de Talleyrand*, Vol. I, English translation by Raphael Ledos de Beaufort, edited by the Duc de Broglie (G. P. Putnam’s Sons, 1891).

**Witness condition:** English translation, not quotation-grade French. All quoted wording is `[V]`: exact wording of the current English project witness, usable to ground sequence, distinctions, and abstraction, but not to license exact French voice imitation.

**Quotation status:** `AUDITED_QUOTATION_GROUNDED`

## 1. Textual ground

> “I do not even understand myself very well when I speak of Philosophers; I employ this term as some people do Nature when they have something vague to say, and the suitable expression is lacking.”
>
> — printed p. 61 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand notices that his own category is too vague and stops before allowing the label to do explanatory work.

### Abstraction

**WHEN EVIDENCE RESISTS A CATEGORY, REPAIR THE CATEGORY BEFORE USING IT AS EXPLANATION.**


## 2. Textual ground

> “If the philosophers… had formed a sect, their doctrine would be easy to understand; but modern philosophy has nothing in common with the spirit of a sect.”
>
> — printed p. 61 `[V]`

### Local reading

A shared label does not establish doctrinal unity.

### Abstraction

**DO NOT INFER DOCTRINAL UNITY FROM A SHARED LABEL.**


## 3. Textual ground

> “Doubt … its danger lies in this, being the aim to which one tends; its advantage, in this being the point of departure.”
>
> — printed p. 63 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand distinguishes doubt used as a destination from doubt used as the beginning of inquiry.

### Abstraction

**USE DOUBT AS A POINT OF DEPARTURE, NOT AS THE AIM.**

## 4. Textual ground

> “intelligence contents itself with examining modestly in order to arrive slowly at causes… from phenomena to phenomena, from discovery to discovery, and finally from truth to truth.”
>
> — printed p. 63 `[V]`

### Local reading

The method he praises proceeds gradually from examination through phenomena toward causes.

### Abstraction

**MOVE SLOWLY FROM PHENOMENA TOWARD CAUSES.**

**DO NOT JUMP DIRECTLY TO CAUSES.**

## 5. Textual ground

> “analysis; useful, when applied to physical sciences; incomplete, when applied to moral sciences; dangerous, when applied to social order.”
>
> — printed p. 66 `[V]`

### Local reading

The same method changes value when moved across objects.

### Abstraction

**LET METHOD ANSWER TO THE OBJECT.**

**DO NOT GIVE A SUCCESSFUL METHOD UNIVERSAL JURISDICTION.**


## 6. Textual ground

> “the object to which the method of analysis shall be applied will point out…”
>
> — printed p. 66 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand makes the object to which analysis is applied part of the judgment of the analyst’s method and title.

### Abstraction

**LET THE OBJECT OF ANALYSIS HELP DETERMINE HOW THE METHOD IS JUDGED.**
