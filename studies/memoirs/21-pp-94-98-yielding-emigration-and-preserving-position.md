# Memoirs Unit 21 — printed pp. 94–98
## Yielding, emigration, and preserving position

**Primary witness:** *Memoirs of the Prince de Talleyrand*, Vol. I, English translation by Raphael Ledos de Beaufort, edited by the Duc de Broglie (G. P. Putnam’s Sons, 1891).

**Witness condition:** English translation, not quotation-grade French. All quoted wording is `[V]`: exact wording of the current English project witness, usable to ground sequence, distinctions, and abstraction, but not to license exact French voice imitation.

**Quotation status:** `AUDITED_QUOTATION_GROUNDED`

**Evidence status:** Independent Memoirs reconstruction. No preexisting deed was consulted or cross-referenced.

## 1. Textual ground

> “there remained but one reasonable course to take, it was to yield without being forced to do so, and while there would still be some credit in doing it.”
>
> — printed p. 94 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand’s immediate criterion is voluntary yielding while some credit can still be retained, once only one reasonable course remains.

### Abstraction

**WHEN ONLY ONE REASONABLE COURSE REMAINS, YIELD BEFORE COMPULSION IF VOLUNTARY YIELDING STILL RETAINS CREDIT.**

## 2. Textual ground

> “retain some influence… and to gain time, which often means to gain everything.”
>
> — printed p. 94 `[V]`

### Local reading

The value of yielding is connected directly to retaining influence and gaining time.

### Abstraction

**DISTINGUISH LOSING A POSITION FROM LOSING ALL INFLUENCE AFTER THE POSITION IS LOST.**

**TREAT TIME AS A POLITICAL ASSET WHEN IT PRESERVES INFLUENCE.**

## 3. Textual ground

> “I did not blame them, but I blamed the emigration. Nearly all the émigrés were actuated by a noble sentiment and by a deep devotion to the royal cause; but emigration was a false step. Whatever may have been its motive … it appeared to me under all these aspects, but a poor calculation.”
>
> — printed p. 95 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand explicitly gives the dominant motive a favorable moral description while judging the political move itself a false step and a poor calculation. The moral character of motive and the prudence of action are separate questions.

### Abstraction

**DISTINGUISH THE CHARACTER OF THE MOTIVE FROM THE PRUDENCE OF THE MOVE.**

## 4. Textual ground

> “Only a portion of them… could go abroad, and this absent portion must inevitably compromise those who remained.”
>
> — printed p. 96 `[V]`

### Local reading

Exit changes the political status and risk borne by those unable to exit.

### Abstraction

**PRICE THE EFFECT OF EXIT ON THOSE WHO CANNOT EXIT.**


## 5. Textual ground

> “But property once lost could not be as easily restored… it might have been transferred…”
>
> — printed p. 96 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand ranks titles and property differently because transferred property may pass through many hands and become practically irrecoverable.

### Abstraction

**RANK LOSSES BY REVERSIBILITY.**

**PAY SPECIAL ATTENTION TO LOSSES THAT TRANSFER ASSETS TO OTHERS.**

## 6. Textual ground

> “Was there a certainty of receiving real assistance, and was it wise to trust simply in hopes?”
>
> — printed p. 97 `[V]`

### Local reading

External support is tested for deliverability rather than treated as an asset because it has been promised or imagined.

### Abstraction

**DO NOT TRADE PRESENT POSITION FOR UNVERIFIED EXTERNAL PROMISE.**


## 7. Textual ground

> “neither the country, its integrity nor its future independence would thereby be injured.”
>
> — printed p. 97 `[V]`

### Local reading

Foreign intervention is unacceptable unless success can occur without injury to the country’s integrity or future independence.

### Abstraction

**TEST FOREIGN HELP AGAINST THE COUNTRY’S POST-SUCCESS INTEGRITY AND INDEPENDENCE.**

## 8. Textual ground

> “not to struggle against a torrent which must be allowed to pass; but to hold myself in a position enabling me to save those who could be saved, to raise no obstacle between opportunity and myself, and to hold myself in readiness for it.”
>
> — printed p. 98 `[V]`

### Local reading

His retrospective self-description is to avoid useless struggle, preserve a position from which some people may still be saved, and remain ready for opportunity.

### Abstraction

**WHEN DIRECT RESISTANCE CANNOT ALTER THE RESULT, PRESERVE POSITION FOR LATER ACTION.**

**DO NOT STRUGGLE USELESSLY AGAINST A TORRENT IF PRESERVING READINESS CAN SAVE SOMETHING LATER.**
