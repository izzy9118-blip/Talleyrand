# Memoirs Unit 06 — printed pp. 21–24
## Reform, control, and the moving board

**Primary witness:** *Memoirs of the Prince de Talleyrand*, Vol. I, English translation by Raphael Ledos de Beaufort, edited by the Duc de Broglie (G. P. Putnam’s Sons, 1891).

**Witness condition:** English translation, not quotation-grade French. All quoted wording is `[V]`: exact wording of the current English project witness, usable to ground sequence, distinctions, and abstraction, but not to license exact French voice imitation.

**Quotation status:** `AUDITED_QUOTATION_GROUNDED`

## 1. Textual ground

> “A particular reform such as this… must necessarily lead to a general attack upon these illustrious bodies.”
>
> — printed p. 21 `[V]`

### Local reading

A reform aimed at one component can alter the conditions of survival of the larger system.

### Abstraction

**BEFORE REFORMING A PART, MAP WHAT THAT PART HOLDS UP.**


## 2. Textual ground

> “Having once dispersed all that learned staff, it became easier to approach the whole ecclesiastical edifice, which, being thus deprived of what constitutes its leading spirit and strength, could not long defend itself with the weapons afforded by the external ceremonies of religion alone.”
>
> — printed p. 21 `[V]`

### Local reading

Talleyrand distinguishes visible institutional form from the cadre that supplies its effective intelligence and defensive capacity.

### Abstraction

**IDENTIFY THE PEOPLE OR CAPABILITIES THAT GIVE AN INSTITUTION ITS OPERATING STRENGTH.**

**DO NOT CONFUSE SYMBOLIC FORM WITH THE CAPACITY THAT MAKES THE FORM DEFENSIBLE.**


## 3. Textual ground

> “They surely believed that they would be able to direct its application, and, if needed, even to suspend it. But the time had already come when people had ceased to be moderate in all questions relative to religious orders.”
>
> — printed p. 22 `[V]`

### Local reading

Those who authorize an instrument may wrongly assume they will continue to control its later application.

### Abstraction

**NEVER PRICE AN INSTRUMENT ONLY BY THE HANDS THAT CREATE IT; PRICE WHO MAY LATER CONTROL IT.**


## 4. Textual ground

> “Hardly a day passed without some work being published… and I do not recollect that… a single clever pen was raised in defence of religious orders.”
>
> — printed p. 22 `[V]`

### Local reading

The asymmetry Talleyrand records is not merely institutional existence but public argumentative force: attacks appeared continually while no comparably able defender answered them.

### Abstraction

**MEASURE WHETHER AN INSTITUTION HAS CAPABLE PUBLIC DEFENDERS, NOT ONLY FORMAL EXISTENCE.**

## 5. Textual ground

> “it was too late; it is true, the pretext was forgotten; besides there was no longer any need of it, since the States-General had become the National Assembly.”
>
> — printed p. 24 `[V]`

### Local reading

The question has moved onto a new board. Solving the earlier pretext no longer solves the later institutional fact.

### Abstraction

**DO NOT SOLVE YESTERDAY’S QUESTION AFTER THE BOARD HAS CHANGED.**

**OPPORTUNITY HAS AN EXPIRATION DATE.**
