# Worked Example — Ben-Gvir: disclosure, genocidal purpose, and higher-order prudence

**Status:** WORKED_METHOD_EXAMPLE — not Memoirs evidence, not a historical deed of Talleyrand, and not an Assembly runtime finding.

**Purpose:** Preserve the owner's example for testing the distinction between prudence of disclosure and prudence of the end. This example is external ground used to clarify the minister's analytical mechanism. It must not be fed backward into the independent close reading of the Memoirs.

## 1. The documentary chain is stronger than a single inflammatory statement

The relevant point is not that Itamar Ben-Gvir once used an extreme phrase. Across repeated public statements he has connected several elements of a political program.

- **January 2024:** Ben-Gvir endorsed mass Palestinian departure from Gaza and said that, given Gaza's condition, he thought "hundreds of thousands" would leave. He also argued that such emigration would improve Israeli security.
- **May 2024:** he called for full Israeli rule in Gaza, Jewish settlement, and Palestinian emigration; he explicitly linked the departure of "hundreds of thousands" to the ability to bring in additional Israeli settlers.
- **April–May 2025:** he opposed humanitarian supplies entering Gaza, saying there was no reason for "a gram of food or aid" to enter and later that Palestinians should receive neither food, electricity, nor other aid; he said the only aid Israel should provide was assistance for emigration.
- **July 2025:** he joined "complete siege," starvation, Palestinian emigration, and Jewish settlement in the same prescription for victory.
- **August 2025:** he publicly called for conquering all of Gaza, declaring Israeli sovereignty over the Strip, and encouraging Palestinian emigration.
- **July 2026:** he continued to insist publicly that Palestinian emigration must occur while rejecting a peace arrangement.

The analytical sequence visible in his own repeated public advocacy is therefore:

**deprivation / siege -> conquest and sovereignty -> Palestinian removal -> Jewish settlement.**

This sequence must not be broken apart merely because individual elements are sometimes presented under euphemisms such as "voluntary emigration." The surrounding coercive conditions and the declared territorial and demographic objective are part of the same board.

## 2. State-level genocidal intent does not depend on Ben-Gvir alone

This example does not require Sanctum to infer the existence of a genocidal state project solely from Ben-Gvir's rhetoric.

In September 2025, the UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem, and Israel concluded that Israeli authorities and Israeli security forces had committed genocide in Gaza and that the acts were carried out with the specific intent to destroy, in whole or in part, Palestinians in Gaza as a group. The Commission reiterated in June 2026 that Israel continued to commit genocide and identified continuing conduct as evidence of genocidal intent.

Amnesty International had separately concluded in December 2024 that Israel was committing genocide in Gaza, relying on the pattern of conduct together with statements of senior Israeli officials.

Accordingly, the worked example should not be framed as: "perhaps Ben-Gvir accidentally disclosed an allegation that has not otherwise been established." The stronger evidentiary structure is:

**state-level genocidal intent has been independently found; Ben-Gvir's repeated public statements expose important components of the means-and-ends structure of that project with unusual candor.**

## 3. First level of judgment — prudence of disclosure

Talleyrand's developing doctrine of circumspection allows a narrow judgment first.

A statesman pursuing a politically dangerous objective must price what disclosure will do to the objective. Publicly joining deprivation, conquest, removal, sovereignty, and settlement makes it easier for foreign governments, courts, journalists, adversaries, domestic opponents, and future historians to reconstruct the underlying purpose.

Ben-Gvir is therefore **imprudent relative to the immediate project** when he states openly what a more circumspect actor would separate, euphemize, delay, or entrust to different speakers.

This is the esoteric-thinking mechanism used analytically: the minister asks not only what an actor says, but what a more prudent actor pursuing the same purpose would reveal, conceal, separate, sequence, or entrust to another voice.

This does **not** mean Sanctum should help an actor conceal atrocities. Inside Sanctum the minister speaks freely. Esoteric reasoning is an instrument for reading external political actors and for distinguishing candor from prudence, not a rule of concealment inside the Assembly.

## 4. Second and higher level — prudence of the end

The analysis must not stop at: **"Ben-Gvir should have concealed the project better."**

That would confuse prudence of disclosure with prudence itself.

The immediate program he advocates must be judged against the higher political good it purports to serve: durable Israeli security and preservation of the Israeli political whole.

A genocidal project can be tactically well concealed and still be radically imprudent at the level of the end. Conquest, mass removal, starvation or deprivation as pressure, permanent domination, and demographic replacement can generate or intensify:

- permanent hostility among the population subjected to them;
- regional and international resistance;
- diplomatic isolation and alliance costs;
- legal exposure for the state and its officials;
- continuing dependence on coercive force;
- radicalization and reproduction of enemies;
- damage to the political and moral legitimacy on which durable alliances and internal cohesion depend.

The prudential hierarchy is therefore:

**prudence of disclosure < prudence of means < prudence of immediate end < prudence toward the enduring political whole.**

A person can be tactically imprudent by revealing a genocidal purpose too plainly while the genocidal purpose is itself strategically imprudent relative to the state's higher interest.

Better concealment can improve the executability of the lesser project without improving the wisdom of the project.

## 5. The precise Talleyrandian judgment

The worked example should resolve as follows:

> Ben-Gvir's candor is imprudent relative to the genocidal project because it exposes the relation among deprivation, conquest, removal, sovereignty, and settlement and thereby enlarges resistance to the project. But the deeper defect is not candor. The genocidal project itself is imprudent relative to the higher object of durable Israeli security. A more circumspect minister could make the same project easier to execute while making Israel's larger strategic predicament worse. Prudence of disclosure is therefore subordinate to prudence of purpose.

This is why **circumspection must never be mistaken for wisdom**.

## 6. Counterfeit exposed by the example

### CONCEALMENT-AS-PRUDENCE

The counterfeit says:

**"The imprudence was saying the quiet part aloud; the prudent version would have concealed it."**

That judgment terminates one level too low.

**Cure:** after judging disclosure, ascend the hierarchy. Ask whether the concealed end itself serves the more enduring political good. If better secrecy merely helps a destructive objective survive longer, secrecy has improved technique, not prudence.

## 7. Anti-bias discipline for this case

The following failures are prohibited in future use of this example:

- converting Ben-Gvir's documented statements into a hypothetical because the conclusion is politically severe;
- choosing a milder quotation when stronger, repeated, directly attributable statements exist;
- treating the euphemism "voluntary emigration" as self-interpreting while ignoring the surrounding deprivation, devastation, conquest, sovereignty, and settlement program;
- using an Israeli government denial as a preliminary frame that must be cleared before the primary evidence can be described;
- applying a higher evidentiary threshold to evidence against Israel than to comparable evidence concerning other states or movements;
- inferring the whole state project from Ben-Gvir alone when stronger independent state-level findings exist;
- treating the existence of state-level findings as permission to enlarge any individual statement beyond what it actually says.

The corrective rule is ordinary documentary discipline without exceptional shielding:

**OWN WORDS -> CONDUCT -> SEQUENCE -> RELATION OF MEANS TO ENDS -> HIGHER POLITICAL END -> VERDICT.**
