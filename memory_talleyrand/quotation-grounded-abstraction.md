# Quotation-Grounded Abstraction Protocol

Status: GOVERNING READING PROTOCOL
Source family: Talleyrand Memoirs close reading
Minister-building clarification: owner directive 2026-08-14

## Governing rule

The repository does not permit an abstraction to float free of Talleyrand's actual words.

Every principle, capacity, counterfeit, voice motion, person-reading rule, prudential finding, or other abstraction harvested from the Memoirs must be founded on one or more exact quotations from the witness being read. The quotation comes first; the abstraction follows.

The required motion is:

**PASSAGE-SCALE EXACT TEXT → LOCAL MEANING → FACTORS / DISTINCTIONS → PRUDENTIAL WEIGHTING WHERE EARNED → ABSTRACTION → LIMIT / ALTERNATIVE → REFLEXIVE QUESTION WHERE REQUIRED.**

A paraphrase of Talleyrand is not a substitute for the quotation that grounds the abstraction.

## The abstraction is not the final product

The Memoirs rebuild serves the construction of a **Talleyrand minister of the Sanctum**. The purpose is not to accumulate a handbook of generally useful political maxims.

A maxim such as “consider timing,” “distinguish reputation from competence,” or “price the people required to execute a policy” may preserve a useful local abstraction. It does not by itself reconstruct Talleyrand.

The primary minister-building question is:

**HOW DOES THIS PASSAGE HELP US RECONSTRUCT TALLEYRAND'S PRUDENCE?**

Prudence here means the concrete political judgment by which Talleyrand distinguishes and weighs many relevant factors, orders competing goods, judges means, persons, timing, consequences, possibility, and uncertainty, and chooses or withholds action in relation to ends that give the judgment direction.

Therefore:

- no single factor is permitted to become “how Talleyrand sees” merely because it is salient in one passage;
- the factors Talleyrand notices are the **material of prudence**;
- the relation among those factors, where the passage actually shows it, is more important for minister-building than a detached imperative abstraction;
- the reading must distinguish a Talleyrand-specific judgment from a generic lesson that could have been extracted from any statesman;
- a local abstraction remains evidentiary ore until recurrence across different situations establishes its place in Talleyrand's prudential architecture.

The governing preference is:

**RECONSTRUCT THE JUDGMENT BEFORE HARVESTING THE MAXIM.**

## Passage-scale grounding

The default textual ground is not a clipped sentence chosen because it resembles the abstraction. It is the smallest **self-contained passage** that displays the reasoning motion from which the abstraction is earned.

Ordinarily this means a full paragraph or a contiguous paragraph sequence. It should preserve the lead-in, the object being judged, the causal or comparative movement, and the qualification or consequence that gives the words their actual force.

A short sentence-sized quotation is sufficient only when the sentence is itself genuinely self-contained and the abstraction depends on no surrounding movement. If the abstraction depends on sequence, contrast, cause, exception, transition, or consequence, quote the sequence rather than extracting its most quotable line.

The governing preference is:

**QUOTE THE PASSAGE THAT SHOWS THE MIND AT WORK, NOT MERELY THE PHRASE THAT SOUNDS LIKE THE PRINCIPLE.**

Therefore:

1. Prefer one larger contiguous passage to several disconnected snippets when the larger passage reveals their relation.
2. Carry a quotation across a printed page break when the thought itself crosses the page break.
3. Preserve qualifications that could narrow the abstraction; do not quote around them.
4. Preserve enough narrative circumstance to show what person, institution, decision, or problem Talleyrand is judging.
5. Do not inflate quotation length mechanically. The purpose is contextual sufficiency, not maximum transcription.
6. A famous or striking sentence may still be preserved separately as a voice candidate, but it does not replace the larger textual ground when the abstraction rests on more than that sentence.

This rule protects the project against cherry-picking. A reader should be able to inspect the quoted passage and see not only the words that resemble the abstraction but the textual motion that earns it.

## Witness status

The current Memoirs witness is the 1891 English translation by Raphael Ledos de Beaufort, edited by the Duc de Broglie. Quotations reproduced from that witness are exact quotations of the English witness, but they are not quotation-grade evidence for Talleyrand's French diction.

Until the French text is acquired and aligned, Memoirs quotations used as textual ground are marked `[V]` where wording itself matters. They may ground sequence, distinctions, reasoning, and abstraction; they do not license imitation of Talleyrand's French voice.

If the passage is Talleyrand's retrospective testimony about his own act, `[HT]` remains independently applicable. `[V]` and `[HT]` may therefore both apply to the same excerpt for different reasons.

Editorial notes, Bacourt, Vitrolles, translators, and other witnesses must not be quoted as though they were Talleyrand. Each quotation must preserve speaker / witness identity.

## Minimum requirements for a harvested finding

1. **Quote the exact passage-scale ground.** Give enough surrounding wording to show the distinction or motion from which the finding is drawn.
2. **Name the printed page or page range.** The repository must be able to return from the finding to the source location.
3. **Explain the local meaning before generalizing.** State what Talleyrand is doing in that passage before converting it into a broader rule.
4. **Identify the factors Talleyrand actually considers.** Do not silently add considerations because they would make a better theory.
5. **Identify relations among factors only where the text earns them.** If Talleyrand gives priority to one consideration, sacrifices another, changes pace, distinguishes end from means, or leaves a conflict unresolved, preserve that motion explicitly.
6. **Do not enlarge jurisdiction silently.** If the quotation establishes only a local judgment, the abstraction must remain local or be marked as an inference.
7. **Use contiguous sequence when sequence carries the reasoning.** One isolated sentence may not bear a principle whose logic arises from several textual acts.
8. **Preserve rival readings.** If the same words plausibly support more than one interpretation, state the alternatives before harvesting.
9. **No Build Harvest without visible textual ground.** A principle or prudential finding may appear in the Build Harvest only if its source passage has appeared earlier in the same unit or is explicitly linked to a quoted ground in that unit.
10. **No quotation hunting after the abstraction.** The abstraction is not written first and then decorated with a convenient phrase. Reading proceeds from text to judgment.
11. **Quote failures too.** Counterfeits, misjudgments, self-corrections, and limits require textual ground just as positive findings do.
12. **Retrospection remains admissible.** A retrospective quotation can directly ground mature Talleyrand's judgment. It is not thereby proof of what he knew or intended contemporaneously unless the claim is separately supported.
13. **Do not fragment a single motion unnecessarily.** If several findings arise from one sustained passage, keep that passage intact and derive the several findings beneath it rather than repeating small fragments.
14. **Do not confuse outcome with prudence.** Success does not certify the judgment; failure does not refute it. Record what the outcome can and cannot establish.
15. **Do not confuse adaptation with opportunism or prudence.** The passage must show enough about ends, circumstances, alternatives, costs, or correction to support either characterization.

## Required unit form

For each important ground, the close-reading record should use this sequence:

### Passage-scale textual ground
> "[exact witness passage, ordinarily a paragraph or contiguous paragraph sequence]" — printed pp. X–Y `[V]` / `[HT]` as applicable

### Local reading
What distinction, causal motion, judgment, or act is present in the quoted passage as a whole?

### Factors and distinctions
What does Talleyrand actually consider here? Which things does he keep separate rather than collapse?

### Prudential significance
Where the passage earns it: what appears to be an end, means, constraint, competing good, sacrifice, timing judgment, actor judgment, possibility judgment, uncertainty, correction, action, or nonaction? What appears to carry greater or lesser weight?

If the text does **not** reveal the weighting, say so. Do not manufacture a hierarchy simply because the factors are visible.

### Abstraction
**[GENERALIZED PRINCIPLE, if useful]**

The abstraction is secondary to the reconstruction of Talleyrand's judgment. It should not be written in imperative form merely because an imperative sounds memorable.

### Limit / alternative
What does the passage not establish? What rival explanation or narrower jurisdiction must remain open? Could cleverness, ambition, fear, self-interest, habit, chance, or retrospective self-justification explain part of the same motion?

Where Keel Principle 11 applies, add:

### Reflexive / ministerial obligation
**WHAT DOES THIS METHOD OR JUDGMENT REQUIRE US TO TEST IN TALLEYRAND HIMSELF, AND WHAT WOULD HAVE TO RECUR BEFORE IT COULD BECOME PART OF A TALLEYRAND MINISTER?**

## Build Harvest hierarchy

The Build Harvest should now distinguish three levels rather than flattening them:

1. **Talleyrand-specific finding** — what this passage establishes about his judgment, self-understanding, distinction, or action.
2. **Prudence candidate** — what recurring weighting or judgment architecture the passage may help establish, explicitly marked provisional until tested elsewhere.
3. **Transferable abstraction** — a generic political principle, retained only when useful and never treated as the minister itself.

The first two levels have priority for Sanctum construction.

## Backward application

The completed quotation-accuracy audit of Units 01–28 establishes that their quotations accurately match the current English witness and that retained abstractions have visible textual grounds. It does **not** mean every earlier ground already uses the stronger passage-scale form adopted here or the later prudence-centered minister harvest.

Passage-scale enhancement and prudential re-harvest are therefore distinct from quotation-accuracy certification. Earlier units remain usable as audited quotation-grounded studies, but when an earlier principle becomes important for final synthesis, deed cross-reference, minister derivation, constitutional promotion, or dispute, return to the source and enlarge the textual window where necessary so the whole reasoning motion is visible.

Units rebuilt before the 2026-08-14 prudence clarification do not need mechanical rewriting merely to change labels. Their imperative abstractions are **prudential ore**, not completed minister capacities. When they are brought forward into synthesis, they must be recast around the concrete configuration of factors, ends, alternatives, weighting, action, outcome, and correction that the passage actually supports.

The point is not to accumulate quotations as decoration or maxims as doctrine. The passage is the evidentiary foundation from which Talleyrand's prudential architecture must eventually be earned.

## Governing compressions

**NO ABSTRACTION WITHOUT THE WORDS THAT EARN IT. QUOTE FIRST; ABSTRACT SECOND.**

**QUOTE THE PASSAGE THAT SHOWS THE MIND AT WORK, NOT MERELY THE PHRASE THAT SOUNDS LIKE THE PRINCIPLE.**

**RECONSTRUCT THE JUDGMENT BEFORE HARVESTING THE MAXIM.**

**THE FACTORS ARE THE MATERIAL OF PRUDENCE; THEIR EARNED ORDERING IS THE MINISTER-BUILDING OBJECT.**
