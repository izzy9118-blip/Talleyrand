# Quotation-Grounded Abstraction Protocol

Status: GOVERNING READING PROTOCOL
Source family: Talleyrand Memoirs close reading

## Governing rule

The repository does not permit an abstraction to float free of Talleyrand's actual words.

Every principle, capacity, counterfeit, voice motion, person-reading rule, or other abstraction harvested from the Memoirs must be founded on one or more exact quotations from the witness being read. The quotation comes first; the abstraction follows.

The required motion is:

**PASSAGE-SCALE EXACT TEXT → LOCAL MEANING → ABSTRACTION → LIMIT / ALTERNATIVE → REFLEXIVE QUESTION WHERE REQUIRED.**

A paraphrase of Talleyrand is not a substitute for the quotation that grounds the abstraction.

## Passage-scale grounding

The default textual ground is not a clipped sentence chosen because it resembles the abstraction. It is the smallest **self-contained passage** that displays the reasoning motion from which the abstraction is earned.

Ordinarily this means a full paragraph or a contiguous paragraph sequence. It should preserve the lead-in, the object being judged, the causal or comparative movement, and the qualification or consequence that gives the words their actual force.

A short sentence-sized quotation is sufficient only when the sentence is itself genuinely self-contained and the abstraction depends on no surrounding movement. If the abstraction depends on sequence, contrast, cause, exception, transition, or consequence, quote the sequence rather than extracting its most quotable line.

The governing preference is:

**QUOTE THE PASSAGE THAT SHOWS THE MIND AT WORK, NOT MERELY THE PHRASE THAT SOUNDS LIKE THE PRINCIPLE.**

Therefore:

1. Prefer one larger contiguous passage to several disconnected snippets when the larger passage reveals their relation.
2. Carry a quotation across a printed page break when the thought itself crosses the page break.
3. Preserve qualifications that could narrow the abstraction; do not quote around them.
4. Preserve enough narrative circumstance to show what person, institution, decision, or problem Talleyrand is judging.
5. Do not inflate quotation length mechanically. The purpose is contextual sufficiency, not maximum transcription.
6. A famous or striking sentence may still be preserved separately as a voice candidate, but it does not replace the larger textual ground when the abstraction rests on more than that sentence.

This rule protects the project against cherry-picking. A reader should be able to inspect the quoted passage and see not only the words that resemble the abstraction but the textual motion that earns it.

## Witness status

The current Memoirs witness is the 1891 English translation by Raphael Ledos de Beaufort, edited by the Duc de Broglie. Quotations reproduced from that witness are exact quotations of the English witness, but they are not quotation-grade evidence for Talleyrand's French diction.

Until the French text is acquired and aligned, Memoirs quotations used as textual ground are marked `[V]` where wording itself matters. They may ground sequence, distinctions, reasoning, and abstraction; they do not license imitation of Talleyrand's French voice.

If the passage is Talleyrand's retrospective testimony about his own act, `[HT]` remains independently applicable. `[V]` and `[HT]` may therefore both apply to the same excerpt for different reasons.

Editorial notes, Bacourt, Vitrolles, translators, and other witnesses must not be quoted as though they were Talleyrand. Each quotation must preserve speaker / witness identity.

## Minimum requirements for a harvested abstraction

1. **Quote the exact passage-scale ground.** Give enough surrounding wording to show the distinction or motion from which the abstraction is drawn.
2. **Name the printed page or page range.** The repository must be able to return from the abstraction to the source location.
3. **Explain the local meaning before generalizing.** State what Talleyrand is doing in that passage before converting it into a broader rule.
4. **Do not enlarge jurisdiction silently.** If the quotation establishes only a local judgment, the abstraction must remain local or be marked as an inference.
5. **Use contiguous sequence when sequence carries the reasoning.** One isolated sentence may not bear a principle whose logic arises from several textual acts.
6. **Preserve rival readings.** If the same words plausibly support more than one interpretation, state the alternatives before harvesting.
7. **No Build Harvest without visible textual ground.** A principle may appear in the Build Harvest only if its source passage has appeared earlier in the same unit or is explicitly linked to a quoted ground in that unit.
8. **No quotation hunting after the abstraction.** The abstraction is not written first and then decorated with a convenient phrase. Reading proceeds from text to principle.
9. **Quote failures too.** Counterfeits and limits require textual ground just as positive principles do.
10. **Retrospection remains admissible.** A retrospective quotation can directly ground mature Talleyrand's judgment. It is not thereby proof of what he knew or intended contemporaneously unless the claim is separately supported.
11. **Do not fragment a single motion unnecessarily.** If several abstractions arise from one sustained passage, keep that passage intact and derive the several abstractions beneath it rather than repeating small fragments.

## Required unit form

For each important harvested principle, the close-reading record should use this sequence:

### Passage-scale textual ground
> "[exact witness passage, ordinarily a paragraph or contiguous paragraph sequence]" — printed pp. X–Y `[V]` / `[HT]` as applicable

### Local reading
What distinction, causal motion, judgment, or act is present in the quoted passage as a whole?

### Abstraction
**[GENERALIZED PRINCIPLE]**

### Limit
What does the passage not establish? What rival explanation or narrower jurisdiction must remain open?

Where Keel Principle 11 applies, add:

### Reflexive obligation
**WHAT DOES THIS METHOD REQUIRE US TO EXAMINE IN TALLEYRAND HIMSELF?**

## Backward application

The completed quotation-accuracy audit of Units 01–28 establishes that their quotations accurately match the current English witness and that retained abstractions have visible textual grounds. It does **not** mean every earlier ground already uses the stronger passage-scale form adopted here.

Passage-scale enhancement is therefore distinct from quotation-accuracy certification. Earlier units remain usable as audited quotation-grounded studies, but when an earlier principle becomes important for final synthesis, deed cross-reference, constitutional promotion, or dispute, return to the source and enlarge the textual window where necessary so the whole reasoning motion is visible.

The point is not to accumulate quotations as decoration. The passage is the evidentiary foundation from which the abstraction is earned.

## Governing compressions

**NO ABSTRACTION WITHOUT THE WORDS THAT EARN IT. QUOTE FIRST; ABSTRACT SECOND.**

**QUOTE THE PASSAGE THAT SHOWS THE MIND AT WORK, NOT MERELY THE PHRASE THAT SOUNDS LIKE THE PRINCIPLE.**
