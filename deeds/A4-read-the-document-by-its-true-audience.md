# DEED A4 — READ THE DOCUMENT BY ITS TRUE AUDIENCE

## MOTION

Begins when a document is offered as though its immediate recipient exhausted its
purpose; ends in a verdict identifying the audience or audiences the document was
built to move and the function it performs for each — or in an explicit refusal to
assign a hidden audience when the ground does not support one.

## WALK

Start with the document as an instrument, not a container. Record who receives it,
who may predictably read it next, what institutional or public record it enters, and
what the writer says or does that bears on those audiences. Separate **possible
secondary readers** from an asserted **true audience**: possibility is not proof.
Where the source itself shows deliberate multi-audience construction, follow the
construction into the wording, omissions, facts selected, and action expected from
each reader. Where it does not, stop at the immediate recipient. The verdict names
the document's operative audience and function without inventing a concealed teaching.

TAL-DISCOVERY-001 governs the chain. A true-audience claim without supporting source
references is a hard failure.

## NEGATIVE SPACE

Reading only for propositional content; assuming the named addressee is the only
reader; treating every diplomatic document as secretly aimed elsewhere; converting
an analyst's theory of audience into documentary fact.

## GROUND

Letter IX supplies the initial problem: Castlereagh's Poland memorandum is read by
function as well as text, but that instance alone was too thin. Letter XVII thickens
the capacity decisively: Talleyrand describes a note to Metternich as composed with
possible reading in England and France in view, and treats wording as selected for
those wider effects. Letter XXIII adds a public form: a circumstantial but restrained
account built so the facts themselves produce reflection in readers. Q3 remains a
limit: deliberate multi-audience construction is documented; a special hidden
King-only teaching layer in the royal dispatches is not established.

## WORKED PROOF

A minister receives a memorandum addressed to him and notices that its argument is
oddly overbuilt for the immediate exchange. He does not write, "the real audience is
the press." He first establishes that the drafter circulated the same instrument to
a legislative committee and instructed an aide that publication was expected. Only
then does the verdict move: the memorandum is simultaneously a negotiation and a
future public alibi, so concessions made for the private reader must be distinguished
from sentences written to survive the public one. Without that second chain, the
hidden audience is imagination and the deed does not fire.
