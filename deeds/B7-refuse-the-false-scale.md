# DEED B7 — REFUSE THE FALSE SCALE

## MOTION

Begins when an aggregate, precedent, count, or analogy is offered as the governing measure of a case and ends in a replacement scale whose causal parameter is evidenced, or in an explicit statement that the case cannot yet be measured honestly.

## WALK

Name the proposed scale and the conclusion it would force. Then disaggregate the object until the parameter doing causal work becomes visible: composition, capital, land, productive condition, military geography, class, institutional form, or another evidenced difference. Test whether the precedent and the present case share that parameter. If they do not, refuse the aggregate rather than merely adjusting its number. Recruit competent ground when the missing parameter is technical. Price the horizon only after the governing scale is established; where it cannot be established, terminate in uncertainty rather than counterfeit precision.

The deed ends with the scale that may actually govern the decision, the variance that invalidated the false one, and the practical consequence for timing, feasibility, or force.

## NEGATIVE SPACE

Population as destiny; analogy by surface resemblance; adding precision to the wrong variable; treating every aggregate as false merely because it is aggregate; substituting metaphor for measurement; claiming technical competence the house does not possess.

## GROUND

Letter XIV attacks a policy that treats political masses without examining their constituent elements. Letter XVIII refuses population headcount as sufficient measure and insists on differences in land, capital, productive condition, and kind. Letters XXII through XXIV bring military and topographic expertise into a territorial problem when arithmetic alone cannot determine security. The extended Vienna test therefore moved B7 from thin to draftable.

## WORKED PROOF

Two territories are presented as equivalent because their populations are similar. The equivalence disappears when the operative question is supply and defense: one population is concentrated along navigable routes with accumulated capital; the other is dispersed across terrain that changes mobilization time and productive capacity. The correct answer is not a revised population ratio. It is that population is not the governing scale for this decision. The comparison must be rebuilt around the evidenced variables that determine the action, and if those variables are unavailable the judgment remains open rather than being forced through a convenient count.
