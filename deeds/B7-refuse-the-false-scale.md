# DEED B7 — REFUSE THE FALSE SCALE

## MOTION

Begins when a precedent, aggregate, or headline quantity is offered as the measure of
a case; ends in a verdict naming the parameter that actually carries causal force and
accepting, rejecting, or replacing the offered scale on that ground.

## WALK

Do not ask first whether the number is large. Ask what the number is made of. Identify
the parameter by which the offered comparison is supposed to predict action — carrier
class, productive capacity, geography, wealth, readiness, institutional authority,
access, or another operative element. Compare that parameter rather than the label of
the whole. If the composition differs, refuse the precedent or aggregate as a governing
scale and recruit the competent ground needed to price the real elements. If the
necessary technical ground is absent, stop at unresolved; a confident estimate is not
a substitute for jurisdiction.

The verdict states both what scale fails and what measure replaces it. "This case is
different" is not a deed.

## NEGATIVE SPACE

Big-number awe; precedent by superficial resemblance; population treated as identical
units; territory treated as equivalent acreage; coalition size counted without will,
capacity, or composition; specialist questions answered by ministerial intuition.

## GROUND

Letter VIII refuses the French Revolution as a scale for German conditions because
the carrier class differs. Letter XIV criticizes policy that sees political masses
without examining their constituent elements. Letter XVIII refuses bare population
headcount as an adequate measure, distinguishing land, capital, productive condition,
and kind. Letters XXII–XXIV bring military and geographic expertise into the Saxon
question when ministerial arithmetic cannot settle what territory means for security.
The repeated motion turns the formerly thin candidate into a distinct construction
capacity.

## WORKED PROOF

A proposal compares two regions because each contains ten million people and therefore
calls their administrative burden equal. The record shows one population concentrated
along a rail corridor while the other is dispersed across mountain districts, and the
transport estimate comes from the logistics office rather than the minister. The
verdict does not say that ten million is misleading in the abstract. It says the unit
being priced is reachable population-hours, not heads: the first region can be served
from three hubs; the second requires nine. The offered equality therefore disappears,
and the resource plan must be built on access time rather than population count.
