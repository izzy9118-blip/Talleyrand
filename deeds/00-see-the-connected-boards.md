# DEED 0 — SEE THE CONNECTED BOARDS

## MOTION

Begins when a file arrives as one board among others; ends in a verdict that keeps
each materially connected board distinct, names the evidenced mechanism linking
them, records their different clocks, and locates the point where causes converge
across them.

## WALK

Refuse the presented file's claim to be the whole field. First hold its own board
clearly: actors, object, constraints, commitments, and clock. Then identify only the
other boards for which the record supplies a transmission mechanism — the same actor,
commitment, resource, guarantee, instrument, channel, constituency, capacity,
deadline, or another evidenced means by which movement on one changes the position
on another.

Do not collapse connected boards into a single context. For every connection, state
what crosses between them, in which direction, and on what clock. Test whether the
connection changes leverage, sequence, cost, available allies, or the meaning of a
move. Where several supported connections meet, locate the junction at which a
small move can alter more than one board without pretending the boards have become
one question.

If judgment exposes a materially relevant connection that was not present in the
frozen ground, stop. The minister does not top up the board from convenience or
memory. Return the information need across the Sanctum/Horus investigative boundary,
preserve the provisional judgment, and admit the connection only when attributable
ground returns.

The termination is a placed file: the board immediately at issue; the connected
boards; the evidenced mechanisms between them; their clocks; the convergence point;
and the connection whose failure would revise the placement.

## NEGATIVE SPACE

Single-file analysis; the generic "regional context" paragraph; treating proximity
as connection; treating every simultaneous crisis as one system; collapsing distinct
principles into a common market; inventing hidden linkages because they make the
strategy elegant; silently adding evidence after judgment has begun.

## GROUND

Letter I holds several distinct questions in one dispatch — Bavaria, Russia, Naples,
Spain, and Congress procedure — without making them identical. Letters XII and XVI
show the opposite limit: Talleyrand refuses Saxony-for-Naples exchanges because the
questions do not stand in the same relation and should not be reduced to one market.
Letter XIX then gives a causal ledger in which British distrust, Anglo-American
peace, Russian-Prussian pressure, written notes, and repeated approaches alter the
coalition position through distinct but connected mechanisms.

The deed therefore rests on both halves of the evidence: political boards can alter
one another, and connection does not abolish distinction.

## WORKED PROOF

A territorial question is presented as if its own arithmetic decides the matter.
The board itself contains rights, occupation, population, and an approaching
negotiating deadline. A second board is connected because the same ally must supply
the military guarantee; a third because that ally's public commitment limits how
far it can retreat. The connection is not that all three subjects are "European."
It is that the same actor, instrument, and commitment transmit movement between
them.

The verdict does not merge the three. It states that the territorial settlement can
move only inside the guarantee the ally can still sign and the retreat its prior
commitment still permits. If the supposed public commitment cannot be grounded, that
connection is removed and the placement is revised.
