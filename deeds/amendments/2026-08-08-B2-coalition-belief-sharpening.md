# B2 INTERPRETIVE SHARPENING — RECONSTRUCT ADHERENCE BEFORE PRICING BELIEF

## Owner act

During substantive review of B2, the owner authorized the recommendation to ratify
B2 with this sharpening. The frozen B2 deed text is not rewritten.

## Governing interpretation

A coalition is neither presumed unitary nor presumed unreal. Before B2 identifies a
belief lever, the analysis must reconstruct each member separately from the available
ground.

For each member, examine as the evidence permits:

- material interests and desired outcomes;
- capacity to remain, defect, delay, obstruct, or impose costs;
- treaty, institutional, legal, or political obligations;
- fears, dependencies, vulnerabilities, and alternatives;
- sunk commitments and prior public positions;
- beliefs about the other members and about the opposing side;
- the connected boards through which those factors reach present conduct.

Only after that reconstruction may the deed ask which part of adherence is actually
belief-dependent. A member may remain because of coercion, dependence, obligation,
material interest, institutional lock-in, fear, lack of alternatives, or some
combination of these even while holding beliefs unfavorable to the coalition.

## Belief lever

The phrase "cheapest belief to change" does not authorize deception or manufactured
appearance. It means the least costly **truthful** change in evidence, commitment,
conduct, demonstrated capacity, or other attributable ground capable of changing the
relevant expectation.

The analysis must state the causal link:

1. what the member presently believes;
2. why that belief matters to its adherence or conduct;
3. what truthful evidence or action could alter the belief;
4. through what channel the new ground reaches the member;
5. what observable conduct should change if the inference is right;
6. by what date that change should become visible.

## Coalition cohesion

B2's negative space prohibits treating a coalition as a unitary actor without
analysis. It does not presume that every coalition is merely apparent. Deep analysis
may conclude that a coalition is genuinely cohesive when common interests,
commitments, institutions, dependencies, or beliefs make collective adherence robust.

If the coalition would remain substantially intact after the proposed belief change,
B2 has not found the governing lever and must say so.

## Vienna application

Letter VI supports the distinction: Talleyrand judged the four-power union more
apparent than real because different members doubted different things about French
means and will. Letter VIII separately treats Austria's desire for French cooperation
and its doubt that France could be counted upon. These are member-specific judgments,
not a license to explain every coalition primarily through belief.

The dated leak prediction remains mandatory. It forces the reconstruction to risk
falsification rather than merely explain events after the fact.
